3 · A low-emission day, hour by hour

Winter AQI is worst early morning and late evening, and usually eases mid-afternoon. Plan around it — you breathe less pollution and create less of it.

5:30 – 8 AM

Smog peak — stay in

Cold air traps pollution near the ground. Skip the early run. Yoga, stretching or indoor workout instead. Keep windows shut.

8 – 11 AM

Commute smarter

Metro/bus over car where you can. If driving, keep windows up, AC on recirculate, and avoid idling at signals and school gates.

12 – 4 PM

Your clean-air window

Usually the day's best air. This is the time for walks, kids' outdoor play, drying clothes outside — and a short 10-minute home ventilation if AQI is below 200.

4 – 7 PM

Errands, batched

Combine trips into one. Walk short distances in this window rather than after dark. Carry a well-fitting N95 if AQI is above 200.

7 – 10 PM

Cook clean, seal the home

Use the exhaust/chimney while cooking, prefer lids on pans, avoid incense and candles on bad days. Close windows as AQI climbs again.

Night

Sleep in clean air

Run the purifier in bedrooms on auto/turbo for the first hour, then quiet mode. No purifier? Keep the bedroom door closed and damp-dust surfaces.

All day

Cut what you add

No waste burning, ever. Wet-mop instead of dry sweeping. Service vehicles and stoves — a tuned engine and a clean burner emit far less.

Weekly

Small habits, compounding

Wash curtains monthly in smog season, clean purifier pre-filters every 2 weeks, and check AQI before planning weekend sport — not after.

4 · Indoor air hacks, room by room

Most winter exposure happens indoors. These are the fixes that matter — most cost nothing.

Know the scale — India (CPCB)

One scale, six bands. If you remember only two numbers: above 200, protect vulnerable family members seriously; above 300, everyone stays in.

0 – 50

Good

Air is clean. Enjoy the outdoors fully, ventilate freely.

51 – 100

Satisfactory

Acceptable for most. Very sensitive people should watch symptoms.

101 – 200

Moderate

Kids, elderly and asthma patients should shorten heavy outdoor activity.

201 – 300

Poor

Everyone may feel effects. Move exercise indoors, mask up outside.

301 – 400

Very Poor

Health alert. Stay indoors, run purifiers, avoid all outdoor exertion.

401 – 500

Severe

Emergency conditions. Seal the home, essential trips only, N95 always outside.

Masks that actually work

  • N95 / KN95 / FFP2 — your default above AQI 200Filters at least 95% of fine particles when it seals well. Pinch the nose strip; no gaps at the cheeks.
  • Cloth & surgical masks — comfort onlyThey block droplets and dust, but leak most PM2.5. Better than nothing for a 5-minute errand, not protection for a commute.
  • Kids need kid sizesAn adult mask on a child leaks everywhere. Buy child-size KN95s and do a fit check — it should move slightly when they breathe in.
  • Replace, don't wash, disposablesRetire an N95 when breathing gets hard, straps loosen, or after roughly 30–40 hours of use.

Purifiers, without the marketing

  • True HEPA (H13/H14) is the filter that matters"HEPA-type" and ionisers are not the same. Ionisers can create ozone — skip them, especially around kids and asthma.
  • Size it by CADR, not room photosPick a CADR (m³/h) at least ⅔ of the room volume × 5 air changes for smog season. Bigger room = bigger CADR.
  • Placement: bedroom firstYou spend 7–8 hours sleeping. One purifier in the bedroom beats a small one in every room. Keep it 30 cm from walls, doors closed.
  • No purifier? You still have optionsSeal window gaps with tape/cloth, damp-mop daily, keep one "clean room" with the door shut, and ventilate only in the afternoon dip.

Three myths to drop this winter

“Plants purify a room”

Plants are lovely — and you'd need hundreds per room to dent PM2.5. Keep them for joy and humidity, not as your air filter.

“AQI looks fine, so the morning run is fine”

The number you saw may be yesterday's average. Early-morning winter air is routinely the worst of the day — check the hourly reading, not just the daily one.

“Indoor air is safe air”

Without sealing and cleaning, indoor PM2.5 often reaches 60–80% of outdoor levels — plus cooking and incense add their own. Indoor needs active work too.

See a doctor promptly if…

  • Breathlessness at rest, wheezing that doesn't ease with a prescribed inhaler, or lips/fingertips turning bluish — seek emergency care.
  • A child's cough keeps worsening, they're breathing fast, or ribs show with each breath.
  • Chest pain, severe headache, or confusion on a high-AQI day.
  • Asthma reliever use is climbing day after day through smog season — your action plan needs a review.

This assistant gives general information, not medical advice. Follow your doctor's plan first, always.
